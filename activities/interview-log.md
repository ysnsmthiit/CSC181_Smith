# Stakeholder Interview Log

## Interviewee Group #1: MSU-IIT Students
* **Date:** 10/23/2026
* **Method:** Online messaging

| Role | Q&A | What we learned |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **MSU-IIT Student**<br>Primary users who will discover organizations and events | **Q: Where do you currently check for upcoming campus events, and why do you usually miss out on them?**<br>**A:** For updates about upcoming events, whether in my college or in MSU-IIT, I usually rely on social media. In most cases the Messenger app reminds me of events, since announcements and reminders are constantly passed on in each respective group chat. However, some events get overlooked or undermined because other important details dominate the group chat discussions. On other social media platforms, the feed updates almost every time, so some events get refreshed to the point that they never come out of the feed again.<br><br>**Q: If there were a single web app listing all verified MSU-IIT campus activities, what features would make you actually bookmark and use it regularly?**<br>**A:** One of the main features that would be best for a single web app is a filter option for each college so events are easily tracked, plus a listing of events for the whole MSU-IIT. Of course, a reminder notification for upcoming events, depending on which college you are in, would also be important.<br><br>**Q: Would seeing a count of how many students clicked 'Going' on an event make you more likely to attend?**<br>**A:** Yes. In almost all cases, students decide whether or not to go to an event based on their friends or how many people will attend, since an event is supposed to be engaging, exciting and enjoyable while also letting you learn new things. So a count of how many people will attend the program or event would be most beneficial in deciding whether or not I will join. | • Events get missed because Messenger group chat announcements are buried under other discussions, and social media feeds refresh so fast that posts disappear.<br>• Wants one web app with a filter per college plus a listing of events for the whole MSU-IIT.<br>• Wants reminder notifications for upcoming events based on their college.<br>• A visible 'Going' count would influence attendance, since students decide based on who and how many are attending. |

### How it Changes Our Proposal
* Add a filter by college alongside an "All MSU-IIT" view, so students can see events from their own college and campus-wide events in one place.
* Add reminder notifications for upcoming events, based on the student's college.
* Keep the "Going" RSVP and show the count on every event, since students decide based on how many people will attend.
* List events in a dedicated, date-sorted calendar instead of a feed, so events don't get buried under group chat messages or refreshed posts.

## Interviewee Group #2: Accredited Student Organizations
* **Date:** 10/21/2026
* **Method:** Face-to-face interview

| Role | Q&A | What we learned |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Orions Dancer A**<br>Primary users who will post profiles and events and want better turnout | **Q: How do you currently announce your events to non-members, and what is the hardest part about getting good student turnout?**<br>**A:** We post on Facebook and send announcements to Messenger group chats. It is not hard to get the information out; rather, it is a culture problem among students, who have a culture of not going to events.<br><br>**Q: How much extra effort would your team be willing to spend posting events on a new platform alongside your official Facebook page?**<br>**A:** It depends on whether the platform is established and can be trusted. We trust the publication team when it comes to promotion. The hardest part is getting reach.<br><br>**Q: What specific details (e.g., event poster, venue, ticket/registration info, target audience) do you consider essential when posting an announcement?**<br>**A:** Our resources are very limited; we cannot impose liabilities, unlike the college student council. Trust is a really big factor: good quality events make students trust the officers to lead great events, which motivates them to attend future events. | • Getting information out is not the problem (Facebook posts and group chats); the real issue is reach and a student culture of not attending.<br>• Willingness to use a new platform depends on it being established and trusted.<br>• Trust in the organizers and good quality events is what motivates students to attend.<br>• Student orgs have very limited resources, so posting on the platform must be low effort. |

### How it Changes Our Proposal
* Make posting quick and low effort: one short form (poster, venue, date, audience) that officers can fill in using their existing pubmats.
* Show verified/accredited organization profiles so the platform earns the trust that officers say adoption depends on.
* Make events visible to all students across colleges, not just org members, to address the reach problem.
* Add organization profiles with past events, so quality events build student trust over time.
* Keep the platform free and light for department councils and organizations with limited resources.

## Interviewee Group #3: KASAMA and Student Council Officers
* **Date:** 10/21/2026
* **Method:** Face-to-face interview

| Role | Q&A | What we learned |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Kuya L (Student Council Officer)**<br>Major event organizers and student governing bodies hosting campus-wide and college-wide events | **Q: How do KASAMA-SSC and College Executive Councils currently coordinate event schedules to avoid date conflicts across different colleges?**<br>**A:** Based on bookings. For the gym and MPH, it is through the admin building; for CCS-specific venues, it is through the dean's office.<br><br>**Q: What features or admin privileges would your council need to highlight major campus-wide events over smaller org announcements?**<br>**A:** Teasers and postings for big events, with themes and dates all on social media. Word of mouth is also important.<br><br>**Q: How do you collect student feedback or measure student engagement after hosting large campus-wide activities?**<br>**A:** Based on last year, the sign-out requirement serves as the evaluation. | • Schedule conflicts are handled through venue bookings: the admin building for the gym and MPH, and the dean's office for college venues. There is no single shared calendar.<br>• Big events are promoted through teasers, themes, and dates on social media, plus word of mouth, so major events need a way to be featured.<br>• Student feedback is currently gathered through the sign-out evaluation requirement, so a post-event feedback feature could fit this. |
| **BUFI Officer C (CCS Student Officer)**<br>Student organization officer outside the publicity committee | **Q: How do you and your team find out when other student orgs or councils announce events on the same dates as yours so you can avoid scheduling conflicts or empty turnouts?**<br>**A:** Usually, announcements about upcoming events are shared in our group chats, and some are also posted on social media. However, sometimes these announcements get buried under other messages, especially when the group chats are active, or we might not see the social media posts. Because of this, officers may overlook important event schedules and only find out about them later.<br><br>**Q: When your org posts an event, how well do you feel those announcements actually reach students outside your department or college, compared to just staying inside your org's own group chats?**<br>**A:** I think posting announcements on social media is more effective in reaching students outside our college compared to just sharing them in group chats. Group chats usually reach only a specific group of students, while social media posts can reach a wider audience, including students from other colleges who might be interested in joining the event.<br><br>**Q: As an officer outside the publicity team, how do you feel about the current announcement posts and pubmats—do you think they accurately highlight the hard work your committee put into the project, or is there a disconnect in how events are presented to students?**<br>**A:** I think the pubmats do a good job of presenting the important details of an event and making it appealing to students. However, they mostly focus on the event itself and do not always show the effort and preparation behind it. As an officer outside the publicity team, I think the pubmats serve their purpose, but there are still things that students might not see, such as the planning, coordination, and hard work of the committees involved. | • Officers can miss conflicting event schedules because announcements get buried in group chats.<br>• Social media reaches students outside the college better than group chats, supporting a centralized public platform.<br>• Pubmats show event details but not the committees' effort, so event pages could highlight the people and preparation behind an event. |

### How it Changes Our Proposal
* Add a shared calendar showing event dates and venues (gym, MPH, college venues), so councils can spot schedule conflicts before booking.
* Add a featured section for flagship and campus-wide events, with teasers, themes, and dates, so they stand out from smaller org announcements.
* Add a post-event feedback form linked to each event, to complement the sign-out evaluation.
* Let event pages show the organizing committees and preparation behind an event, so officers' effort is visible.
* Make events easy to share on social media, since promotion still relies on social media and word of mouth.

## Interviewee #4: Office of Student Development and Services (OSDS)
* **Date:** 10/21/2026
* **Method:** Face-to-face preliminary walk-in inquiry (OSDS Administrative Staff)

| Role | Q&A | What we learned |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **OSDS Administrative Staff**<br>Regulatory and accrediting body overseeing recognized student organizations and activity permits | **Inquiry:** We visited the OSDS office to ask about organization accreditation, activity permit verification, and how a centralized event portal could align with their office workflows.<br><br>**OSDS Staff Response:** Informed us that formal interviews and office consultations cannot be done through walk-ins. Instructed us to submit a formal request letter (via email or hard copy) addressed to the OSDS Director, and emphasized that we must attach a **Data Privacy and Ethics** plan, noting that data privacy and ethical compliance are required even at the project proposal stage. *(Note: Due to our limited semester timeline, our team did not proceed with submitting a formal letter to the Director, making this walk-in inquiry the extent of our OSDS interaction.)* | • Formal consultations and access to official OSDS processes require a formal request letter addressed to the OSDS Director.<br>• OSDS requires a **Data Privacy and Ethics** plan before engaging with student systems, even for a project proposal.<br>• Administrative approval takes extra lead time that does not fit within a short 8–10 week development window. |

### How it Changes Our Proposal
* **Keep the Prototype Independent of Official OSDS Systems:** Because getting formal approval from the OSDS Director requires extra administrative lead time and formal privacy reviews, our semester prototype will operate independently using manual officer sign-ups rather than official OSDS accreditation rosters.
* **Include Basic Data Privacy Practices:** Even without formal OSDS endorsement, we will include basic data privacy and ethical considerations (such as limiting collected user data to `@g.msuiit.edu.ph` emails and basic event details) in our design.
* **Defer Formal Office Consultation to Future Iterations:** Treat formal outreach to the OSDS Director as a post-semester step if the app is ever pursued for official campus-wide deployment.
